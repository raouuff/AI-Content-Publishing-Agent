# Field Trial Demonstrates Beneficial Soil Fungus R. irregularis Improves Wheat Germination and Soil Organic Carbon

A 90-day field trial conducted from March 3 to June 15, 2025, has demonstrated positive agronomic outcomes from the application of the beneficial soil fungus R. irregularis. Research teams logged a total of 214 field observations across 12 monitoring plots to evaluate early crop establishment and changing soil conditions relative to untreated control plots.
Analysis of the trial data revealed that wheat seedlings treated with R. irregularis achieved a 47 percent higher germination rate by day 14 compared to untreated controls. Soil health indicators also improved over the course of the study, with soil organic carbon in treated plots rising from 2.1 percent to 2.8 percent.
Dr. Lena Farid will present these interim results in a 20-minute talk at the regional agronomy meeting. Following the completion of quality checks in early October, the organization will release the full report and cleaned dataset internally.

- Wheat seedlings treated with R. irregularis achieved a 47 percent higher germination rate than untreated control plots by day 14
- Soil organic carbon in treated plots rose from 2.1 percent to 2.8 percent over the 90-day trial (March 3 to June 15, 2025)
- Research protocol encompassed 12 monitoring plots and 214 total logged field observations
